## Any experience with M450Bprogram/summing amp?

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Aug 26, 2009|42_

> So I had a huge response typed out, and then I-Explorer crashed... Joy! Thank you Windows...
>
> Twisted wires will give very little benifit in this case.
> Yes twisted wires are great at canceling out Induced noise in the wires... but inside of this case there will be very little noise to introduce.
>
> I would personally use old Telephone wire. I strip off the outer jacket, and use the coloured wires inside.
> Cat-5 Internet cable works great too... but I stole, I mean found 300 feet of 50-Pair telephone wire.... Lots of colours!
>
> I would set up the M450's as Summing amplifiers.
> What you put into it, is what you get out... no real need too worry about gain and head-room...
>
> However, a 5-k Logarithmic Pot will work as the Gain-Controll for the Program Amp. Connected backwards too Pins 9 and 10.
>
> If you decide to use the amps as Summing, you will need to attach a 500-ohm pot, or something close to that size, between pins 10 and 11.
> This pot will be used for Fine-Adjustment on the gain... Personally I wouldn't put it on the front of the chassis, it's kind of useless.
>
> You could also connect a 250-Ohm resistor between pins 10 and 11... Instead of using a pot.
> This is simpler, and a bit cheaper. (Any value close will work... It wont be Perfectly Unity-Gain, but I'm sure you wont notice!)
>
> You can use 15K resistors, or you can use 10K resistors... You might even get away with 5K resistors! It doesnt really matter.
> (Well it does from an Impedance sence, but noone will notice the difference in your case.)
>
> I'd use 10K, because I have over 3000 of them in a bag. But use 15K if you have them.
>
> You will need 2 resistors per input. 1 for Pin 2, and 1 for Pin 3.
> You will need a Left Pin-2 Buss, and a Left Pin-3 Buss.
> and then a Right Pin-2 buss, and a Right Pin-3 buss.
>
> (Unless your using Unbalanced inputs, in which case let me know!)
> (I can draw a picture for this if it helps clarify things.)
>
> I would then connect the buss directly to the inputs of the appropriate M450 card... left to left, and right to right.
> (Connecting to pins 3 and 5 on each amp.)
>
> Pin-1 on your input connectors should connect to Ground, also known as 0-Volts, or the Common from your power supply.
>
> This 0-volt point should also have one wire connecting to the metal Chassis. A hole into the side, and a nut-and-bolt work great.
> (If this is still confusing, let me know and I'll draw a picture.... I like drawing!)
>

## More facts... what would you like to have answered?

_WBS INFO · Sep 8, 2009|42_

> CHAPTER 2
>
> From M441 to the M460
>
> The M441 input modules and their associated modules represented the original console design by WBS. These early designs were the Phase 1 designs, begun by Ron Ward and Roger Beck as they formed the company in a "garage", upon their departure from McCurdy Radio. Ron Ward was the "wheeler-dealer" and Roger was the "inventor and father of the product", as the company was formed. Virtually all of these early consoles were custom designs to suit the requests of each of the early customers.
>
> In the mid 1970's CBC put out a request-for-quotation for it's soon to be built Vancouver Headquarters on Cambie Street. It required approximately 30 audio consoles for radio, TV, editing, film, and music studios. WBS won this "plum" and it forced the development of the Phase 2 series of consoles beginning with the M460 input modules.
>
> Console design implemented radical updates and improvements. Console metalwork changed from sheet metal to extrusion techniques, with excesses in strenght. End-bells were machined from 1/4 inch aluminum stock. All horizontal elements were custom designed (RKB) extrusions with a captive nut system. The console bodies sat on massive frames welded from rectangular heavy tube stock. Covers and cable ducts abounded, and the console was attractive from any angle.
>
> Module fabrication changed from engraved plastic front panels to an extrusion with with silk-screened metal inset. Mu-metal module covers minimized crosstalk and RF interference. ASSIGN, EQ, L/C, NG, HP/LP and PS modules were all developed, at the same time, to complete these console designs. The very versitile M450 summing, booster, and output module was also created at this time.
>
> The CBC Vancouver project allowed WBS to produce several consoles of the same design for probabaly the first time (radio and edit console designs).

> I have, in fact, used several of the M450B (haven't looked at previous versions) boards to make mic preamps -
>
> These max out at about 40dB of gain, which is incidentally about the same as some of the line amps which are used as mic pres all the time. 
>
> I've tried two different approaches:
> 1) high-ratio input transformer, with 1:2 output transformer
> 2) two M450B modules in series for a two stage mic pre - somewhere around 30dB gain each, but variable.
>
> Both worked just fine, sound good, and make a good platform to test various opamp chips and transformers.
>
> They obviously make good summing amps, too. 
>
> Cheers
